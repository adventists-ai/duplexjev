# Keeping a speech-decision LLM able to talk

*2026-10-10 · DuplexJev-4B-Para v1.1 · [中文](2026-10-chat-replay_zh.md)*

DuplexJev reads decisions from speech as a **single-token** answer of the LLM. Every new ability (turn-taking, stress,
non-verbal sounds, speaker) is taught with a rank-16 LoRA on multiple-choice questions whose target is one letter.
A team building a voice agent on top of our models reported that, after these LoRAs, the model "answers and stops" and
is hard to use for normal conversation. This note is what we found and how we fixed it.

## 1. What actually happens

Greedy generation, same prompts for every model (spoken qa100 questions, typed chat, a "repeat it in the same tone"
prompt, and 12 held-out multi-turn conversations whose last turn is spoken):

| model | spoken question (mean reply tokens) | spoken question + "explain" | typed chat | multi-turn (cap 300) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32B base (no LoRA) | 89 | 94 | 160 (cap) | — |
| 32B-Turn → Stress → Para | 81 → 75 → 53 | 80 → 77 → 76 | 160 | — |
| 4B base / 4B-Turn / 4B-Para | 86 / 72 / 53 | 71 / 70 / 61 | 160 | 289 / — / 287 |
| 4B-Para, LoRA swapped back to the base LLM | 78 | 69 | 160 | — |

No model stopped after one or two tokens, and multi-turn replies kept their length. But every stacked LoRA made
**single-turn spoken answers shorter and more conclusion-first** ("Xiaogang is the shortest."), about −40 % for Para.
Swapping the LLM weights back restores the length, so the drift lives in the LoRA, not in the connector.

Why: the only non-decision data in the LoRA stage was Ultravox's continuation task, prompted with *"Continue the
following text using less than 50 words"*, so its targets are one sentence long. Decision targets are one letter, and
`max_response_tokens` defaults to 50. The LoRA never saw a long answer.

## 2. Conversation replay, and two bugs on the way

We added a **conversation-replay** set to the LoRA mix: user turns from spoken dialogue data (with up to six earlier
turns as text history), SmoothConv conversational fragments and AISHELL-1 sentences, each paired with the **original
base LLM's own reply** to the transcript (Qwen3-4B, no length limit; replies that call the input "unclear" or
"incomplete" are removed, 38 % of the fragment data).

The first attempt failed badly: qa100 79 → 51, and six of eight spoken questions were answered *"You don't seem to have
entered anything."* The mixed loss we use routes every row whose answer is not a single letter to **KL against a
text-only teacher**, and the teacher's input is the transcript in place of the audio. Our replay rows had an empty
transcript, so the teacher literally saw an empty message, and the student learned its answer to that.

Fixes (open-sourced in [`training/ultravox_patches/chat_replay.diff`](../training/ultravox_patches/chat_replay.diff)):

1. rows with an empty transcript get plain cross-entropy on the whole reply;
2. text-only rows (no `<|audio|>` in the chat) carry one dummy audio chunk that inserts zero tokens, so accelerate's
   batch splitting keeps rows and audio chunks aligned (without it, training crashed with an index error);
3. `max_response_tokens` raised from 50 to 256;
4. **per-row loss averaging** (`ML_ASR_CE_ROWNORM=1`).

The fourth one mattered most for the decision skills:

| 4B run | qa100 | ZJU-ML | emotion | VB OBQA | DEBATE stress | POSN-en | PAIR-pos | StressPresso |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4B-Para v1.0 (no replay) | 79 | 50 | 91.5 | 57.8 | 89.0 | 94.1 | 34.1 | 84.7 |
| replay, token-averaged loss | 78 | 61 | 88.9 | 48.8 | 77.4 | 75.4 | 14.3 | 71.8 |
| replay, **per-row** loss | 78 | 58 | 91.6 | 55.4 | 89.0 | 90.4 | 34.1 | 82.7 |
| + 4.5 k in-house chat items (**released as 4B-Para v1.1**) | 77 | 58 | 90.0 | 55.2 | 89.9 | 92.0 | 28.6 | 84.2 |

With token averaging a 256-token reply carries 256 times the gradient of a one-letter decision, and stress fell 12–20
points. Averaging inside each row first gives every example the same weight and recovers almost all of it.

## 3. What is still open

- Single-turn spoken questions are still answered in ~40–50 tokens (base: ~86). Replay targets are replies to
  statements and dialogue turns; quiz-like spoken questions look like the decision data. A replay set of spoken
  open questions is next.
- v1.1 gives up a little: emotion −1.5, VoiceBench OBQA −2.6, sigh AUC 0.81 → 0.71, breathing in natural
  conversation 0.84 → 0.74.
- "What can you do?" sometimes drifts into generic assistant skills.
- The 32B version (same recipe, replies generated by the Qwen3-VL-32B language model) is training.
